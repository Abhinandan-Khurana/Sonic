==========================================================================================
1. THE IDEA IN ONE BREATH
==========================================================================================

One line
  A free vinyl app: your own albums become records you pull from a crate, drop on a glass
  turntable and play, lossless, and share with friends, with no server behind it.

30 seconds
  Streaming made music endless and weightless. Runout brings back the ritual of records.
  Pick the folder where your albums live and each one becomes a record on your shelf, with
  its real cover printed on the vinyl. You flick through a crate, pull a record out, drag it
  onto a see-through turntable and lower the needle. It plays in full quality, up to
  24-bit/192 kHz, with a pro-grade EQ. Every Friday ten free Creative Commons albums drop,
  and you can claim three. You can find friends by username, see their shelves, and send
  records phone to phone. There's no company server: everything lives on the phones, and the
  little that's public sits on free community relays.

2 minutes (add to the above)
  - It's a collecting app, not a streaming app. You only play what you own: your folder,
    drops you claimed, records friends sent you, and records you pressed yourself.
  - The turntable is the interface. It's modelled on the classic DJ direct-drive deck:
    strobe-dotted platter, S-shaped tonearm, pitch fader, start/stop, 33/45, and a tiny
    pixel screen showing the track. Drop the needle anywhere to jump in the side. Hold the
    record to slow it down. Flip it when side A ends.
  - Sound is taken seriously: hi-res output in 32-bit float, a 10-band EQ, headphone
    correction profiles, loudness levelling, a limiter, and a Pure mode that sends the music
    untouched (bit-perfect to a USB DAC on Android 14+).
  - The Pressing Plant lets you make your own record: pick tracks, design the sleeve, choose
    splatter, marble, clear or picture-disc vinyl.
  - Sharing is serverless: a record travels as a single ".vinyl" file over Quick Share,
    Telegram, WhatsApp or Drive, checked byte for byte on arrival.
  - It's free, has no ads, and costs nothing to run.


==========================================================================================
2. WHY IT EXISTS, AND THE RULES IT LIVES BY
==========================================================================================

The problem
  - Streaming apps are lists. Nothing feels owned, nothing is scarce, nothing is a ritual.
  - People who care about sound often keep lossless files (FLAC) that most players flatten
    to 16-bit or don't present with any care.
  - Music sharing between friends lost its warmth: no more passing a record across a room.

Who it's for
  - People who download or buy albums (Bandcamp, Qobuz, CD rips) and want a beautiful,
    serious player for them.
  - Vinyl lovers who want the feel of records on their phone.
  - Friend groups who like swapping music and seeing each other's collections.
  - Independent artists who want to "press" and give away their own music.

The rules (design principles)
  1. Free, and no server to run or pay for. A solo builder with no budget can keep it alive.
  2. A limited vinyl experience, not unlimited streaming. One record on the platter. Sides
     end. You flip them. Nothing autoplays. Scarcity comes from time and choice, not money.
  3. Your music stays yours. Playback is local and offline from your own folder.
  4. Lossless in, lossless out. Files are never re-encoded. Labels say exactly what a file
     is ("FLAC · 24-bit · 96 kHz"), never guessed upwards.
  5. Rights-safe sharing. Audio travels only for your own music and Creative Commons
     records. Everything else travels as a "card" (sleeve, vinyl design, track list). This is
     what keeps the app publishable on Google Play.
  6. The turntable is the interface. Gestures you'd use on a real deck do the real thing.
  7. Honest about limits. When something can't be done without a server, the app says so.


==========================================================================================
3. WHAT USING IT FEELS LIKE (WALKTHROUGH)
==========================================================================================

First launch
  1. A white room. Glass turntable on the left, an empty crate on the right, tiny labels in
     the corners ("RUNOUT · your room", the time, "Choose a folder to begin").
  2. Tap the empty sleeve and pick your music folder with Android's folder picker. No storage
     permission is needed; the app gets access to that one folder only.
  3. Albums appear in the crate one by one as the folder is read. Each gets its cover: from
     inside the files, from a cover.jpg in the folder, or found online.
  4. If a folder has no music, the empty sleeve says what it found instead
     (for example "found .m4p ×12").

Playing a record
  1. Flick the crate sideways. Sleeves stand in perspective; the one in the middle turns to
     face you and lifts.
  2. Tap the front sleeve. The record slides out (upwards in portrait).
  3. Drag the disc onto the platter. It grows as it nears the spindle and snaps into place.
  4. Lower the tonearm onto the record (drag it, or tap it, or press START/STOP). You hear the
     needle drop and a little crackle, then the music. The record spins with the album cover
     printed on it.
  5. The pixel screen scrolls "A1 TRACK NAME · ARTIST" and shows the time and speed.
  6. Drop the needle anywhere on the record to jump to that point of the side. The gaps
     between tracks show as smooth bands in the grooves.
  7. ◀◀ ▶▶ skip tracks. At the end of a side, ▶▶ turns the record over.
  8. Hold the record with a finger: the music winds down. Let go: it spins back up.
  9. 33/45 keys change speed; the pitch fader nudges it ±8 %.
  10. When side A ends you hear the runout groove; tap the record to flip it to side B.

The four panels (top-right buttons, or bottom row in portrait)
  - Drops: this Friday's ten free albums; claim three.
  - Friends: your account and handle, search people by username, open their profiles and
    shelves, your friends list.
  - Pressing Plant: make your own record.
  - Sound: Pure mode, hi-res output, EQ, ReplayGain, limiter, warmth.

Sending a record
  Pull a record out of the crate and press "Send with audio" (your own music or Creative
  Commons) or "Send as a card" (anything else). Android's share sheet opens: Quick Share,
  Telegram, WhatsApp, Drive. Your friend taps the file and the record lands on their shelf.

4.1 THE LISTENING ROOM AND THE GLASS TURNTABLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[BUILT] Layout
  - Landscape: turntable left, crate right. Portrait: turntable on top, crate below, panel
    buttons along the bottom. The app follows the phone's rotation.
  - Corner labels in a small grotesk (Inter): brand and folder actions top-left; panel
    buttons, clock and record count top-right; "Now spinning ..." bottom-left; a hint
    bottom-right.

[BUILT] The turntable (modelled on the classic direct-drive DJ deck, without any brand)
  - Plinth of frosted, faintly silver glass with real refraction (lens effect) on
    Android 13+, blur on 12, frosted white on older phones.
  - Platter rim with four rows of strobe dots (180 per row) that turn with the platter.
  - Smoked see-through rubber mat with ribs when no record is on.
  - 45-rpm adapter well, top left.
  - Tonearm base: smoked-glass disc, chrome height collar with knurling, anti-skate dial,
    arm-rest post.
  - S-shaped chrome tonearm with ribbed counterweight, offset headshell and finger lift.
    The arm is solved with real geometry (law of cosines) so the stylus follows a true arc
    across the grooves, from the lead-in to the runout.
  - Pitch fader on the right, ±8 % (minus at the top, plus at the bottom, like the real
    deck). A green light shows exactly zero; it clicks to zero near the middle; double-tap
    resets it. Disabled in Pure mode.
  - START/STOP square key with a green "playing" light.
  - 33 and 45 keys with an orange light on the selected one.
  - ◀◀ and ▶▶ keys in the same style.
  - Power knob with a red light when a record is loaded.
  - Name plate: "RUNOUT · GLASS DRIVE RG-1".

[BUILT] The pixel screen
  - A small dot-matrix screen built into the plinth, mint dots on dark glass.
  - Uses the Silkscreen pixel font (open licence) at its native size: one screen dot per
    font pixel, so it looks like a real 8-bit display. Any language still shows (non-Latin
    letters fall back to the phone's font, drawn into the same dots).
  - Top line: track code, title and artist, e.g. "A2 GLASS HOURS · INES VARGA". It scrolls
    one dot every 90 ms while the record plays, and stays still otherwise (saves battery).
  - Bottom line: time in the track and the speed, e.g. "1:23  33 +1.5", or "CUE A1",
    "STOP 1:23", "SIDE A END", "NO RECORD".
  - TalkBack reads the screen aloud.

[BUILT] The records
  - Every album with a cover is pressed as a picture disc: the cover is printed across the
    whole vinyl and turns with the platter, under fine grooves, inside a clear vinyl rim.
  - A light sheen stays still while the record turns under it (that's what makes it read
    as vinyl).
  - Track gaps show as smooth bands; their positions come from the real track lengths.
  - The centre shows "SIDE A" and the quality ("24/96").
  - Albums without a cover get a vinyl colour from their quality: black for lossy files,
    solid colour for lossless, clear coloured vinyl for hi-res.
  - Records made in the Pressing Plant use whatever vinyl the maker chose (black, colour,
    clear, splatter, marble, picture disc).

[BUILT] Gestures and controls
  - Drag the tonearm onto the record: plays from that point. Lift it off: stops.
  - Tap the arm while it's resting: the needle drops at the start of the side.
  - Tap the record (needle up): flips it to the next side.
  - Press and hold the spinning record: music winds down; release: spins back up.
  - START/STOP: lowers the needle where you left off; if playing, stops the platter
    (the needle stays down); press again to start.
  - ◀◀: back to the start of the track, or the previous track if it only just began
    (within 3 s); from the first track of side B it goes to the last track of side A.
  - ▶▶: next track; at the end of a side the record is turned over.
  - 33/45: plays at 1.0× or 1.35× (just for fun). Pitch adds ±8 % on top.
  - Back button closes a panel or puts a pulled record back.

[BUILT] Sides
  - Albums are split into vinyl sides of about 22 minutes, never cutting a track, balanced
    by time. Long albums become double LPs with sides C and D (always an even number).

[BUILT] Sounds of the turntable
  - Needle drop, lift, surface crackle (loop) and runout thumps, all synthesised in code
    (no sound files). Crackle can be switched off. All of it is off in Pure mode.

[BUILT] The crate
  - Sleeves stand in a row in 3D perspective, tilted; the middle one faces you and lifts.
  - Flick with momentum; it snaps to a sleeve.
  - Caption under the front sleeve: title, artist, year, exact quality, position, and where
    it came from in blue ("Sent by Maya", "Friday Drop · CC BY 4.0", "Your pressing ·
    splatter vinyl").
  - Hi-res sleeves carry a round "HI-RES 24/96" sticker. The record that's on the platter
    shows an "On the platter" tag.

[BUILT] Background playback
  - Keeps playing with the screen off; lock-screen and notification controls. The playback
    service only starts the first time a record goes on the platter.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 YOUR MUSIC FOLDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[BUILT] How albums are found
  - You pick one folder with Android's folder picker (Storage Access Framework). The app
    keeps access to that folder only.
  - It walks the folder up to 8 levels deep, skipping hidden folders (names starting with
    a dot).
  - Files it reads as music: FLAC, MP3, WAV, M4A/MP4 (AAC or Apple Lossless), AAC, Ogg,
    Opus.
  - Albums are grouped by the album tag, or by folder when files have no tags.
  - Artist comes from the album-artist tag, else the most common artist.
  - Track order follows disc and track numbers, then file names.
  - Albums appear in the crate one at a time as they're read, not all at the end.
  - The shelf is saved on the phone, so it appears instantly next time.
  - "Rescan" re-reads the folder; "Change folder" switches to another.

[BUILT] Reading the files (Runout's own tag reader)
  - FLAC: stream info (sample rate, bit depth, channels, exact length), Vorbis comments
    (title, artist, album, track, disc, year, licence, ReplayGain), embedded cover.
  - MP3: ID3 v2.2/2.3/2.4 tags, embedded cover, ReplayGain, exact length from the Xing/Info
    or VBRI header, or an estimate for constant bitrate.
  - WAV: format, exact length, LIST INFO tags.
  - Other formats use Android's own metadata reader.
  - A file nothing can read still goes on the shelf with an estimated length. (This was the
    0.1 bug: Android's reader returned nothing for some files and whole albums vanished.)

[BUILT] Covers, in this order
  1. Cover embedded in the files (front cover preferred).
  2. An image in the album folder (cover / folder / front / album / albumart / artwork).
  3. Online: search MusicBrainz by artist and album (only confident matches), then fetch
     the front cover from the Cover Art Archive. One lookup every ~1.1 s, as MusicBrainz
     asks. Every request identifies the app: "Runout/0.3.0 ( https://github.com/Abhiroop1122 )"
     (change it in net/Http.kt).
  4. No match: a typographic sleeve in a pastel colour (title and artist).
  Covers are saved on the phone and their main colour is used to tint the vinyl.

[BUILT] Honest quality labels
  - Exactly what the files are: "FLAC · 24-bit · 96 kHz", "FLAC · 16-bit · 44.1 kHz",
    "MP3 · 320 kbps". Hi-res = lossless and more than 16-bit or more than 48 kHz.

[BUILT] Scan report
  - After a scan: "4 albums from 7 music files in 6 folders", or what went wrong:
    "Looked in 3 folders and found no music files (found .m4p ×12)", or that Android
    didn't let the app list the folder.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 SOUND: LOSSLESS, HI-RES, EQ, PURE MODE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[BUILT] Hi-res output
  - Each file plays at its own sample rate in 32-bit float, up to 24-bit/192 kHz, instead of
    being folded down to 16-bit. (Switchable: "Hi-res output" in the Sound panel.)
  - Why it needed custom work: the player library (Media3) switches off plug-in audio
    effects for float audio. So Runout puts its own audio output stage ("DSP sink") in front
    of Android's: every buffer (16-bit, 24-bit, 32-bit or float) is converted to float,
    processed, and handed to Android as float. Proven in tests: the smallest 24-bit step
    survives, and a 24/96 file reaches the output as 96 kHz float, sample for sample.
  - Only Apache-licensed code (Media3 and Android). No GPL decoder, so the app's own code
    never has to be published.

[BUILT] The processing chain (all in 32-bit float)
  1. ReplayGain: evens out loudness between records using the gain tags in the files.
     Album mode (default), Track mode, or Off.
  2. Automatic headroom (preamp): lowers the level by exactly the largest EQ boost, so
     boosted bands never clip.
  3. EQ:
     - 10-band graphic EQ at 31, 62, 125, 250, 500, 1k, 2k, 4k, 8k, 16 kHz; ±12 dB each.
       Drag a fader; double-tap resets it.
     - Presets: Flat, Warm vinyl, Bass, Vocal, Bright, Loudness, Late night.
     - AutoEq headphone correction: import a "ParametricEQ.txt" from autoeq.app (peak,
       low/high shelf, low/high pass filters with frequency, gain and Q, plus its preamp).
     - Filters are standard studio "biquad" designs, checked in tests against their exact
       frequency response.
  4. Warmth (optional): a gentle low-shelf lift (+1.5 dB at 110 Hz) and a softer top
     (−2 dB above 9 kHz), like a warm cartridge.
  5. Limiter: a look-ahead limiter (1.5 ms) that holds every peak under −0.3 dBFS.

[BUILT] Pure mode
  - Nothing touches the signal: no EQ, ReplayGain, limiter, crackle, 45 rpm, pitch or
    wind-down. The samples pass through bit-identical (checked in tests).
  - On Android 14+ with a USB DAC that supports it, the app asks Android for bit-perfect
    output (no resampling, no mixing). The Sound panel says whether it's on, or why not
    ("Plug in a USB DAC", "This phone or DAC doesn't offer bit-perfect").

[BUILT] Sound panel
  - Shows what's actually reaching the speakers, e.g. "24-bit · 96 kHz → 32-bit float".
  - Toggles: Pure, Hi-res output, EQ, Limiter, Warmth; ReplayGain mode; presets; faders;
    Import AutoEq; automatic headroom readout.

[PLANNED] Sound extras
  - Per-device EQ profiles (switch when different headphones connect).
  - Level-matched A/B when turning the EQ on and off.
  - Send an EQ preset to a friend like a record.
  - Spectrum check that flags "fake hi-res" files (a 24/96 file with nothing above 16 kHz).
  - Records gaining a little crackle the more they're played.
  - Real scratching (music playing backwards as you drag), which needs a custom audio
    engine.

4.4 ACCOUNTS, USERNAMES AND PROFILES, WITH NO SERVER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The problem
  Searching for someone by username means their profile has to be somewhere that's always
  online, even when their phone is off. "No server" can't mean "nowhere". So Runout uses
  free public infrastructure that other people already run, and that can't tamper with
  your data: Nostr relays.

What Nostr is (plain words)
  An open protocol. Anyone can run a "relay", a simple storage box for small signed
  messages. Thousands of apps use the same public relays for free. Every message is signed
  with its author's key, so a relay can store and pass on your profile but can't change it
  or fake one. Runout uses three well-known relays: relay.damus.io, nos.lol and
  relay.primal.net (checked to be reachable and to support the searches Runout makes).

[BUILT] Your account
  - "Make your account": pick a username, a display name and a colour.
  - The phone creates a secret key (a 256-bit number) and the public key that goes with it.
    The secret key never leaves the phone.
  - Your profile is a small signed message stored on all three relays:
    username, display name, colour, and your shelf (title, artist, year, quality and sleeve
    colour of up to 600 albums).
  - The profile refreshes itself a few seconds after your shelf changes, and once per app
    start.
  - "Show my records on my profile" can be switched off (the shelf is then left out).
  - The app works offline too: the account is made on the phone and the profile goes up
    next time you're online.

[BUILT] Usernames and the 4-character code
  - Usernames: 3 to 20 characters, lowercase letters, numbers, _ and . (no dot at either
    end, no two dots in a row). Typed in any case; stored lowercase.
  - Without a central server, nobody can reserve a name. So every handle carries the first
    four characters of its public key: @abhi·7F3A. Two people who both pick "abhi" show up
    as @abhi·7F3A and @abhi·C21B. Friends confirm the code.

[BUILT] Search
  - Type the start of a username: "abh" finds @abhi and @abhiroop. Typing a full handle
    ("@abhi·7F3A" or "abhi#7f3a") finds exactly that person.
  - How prefix search works without a server: each profile is tagged with every prefix of
    its username (ab, abh, abhi), and relays can look up tags instantly.
  - Every result's signature is checked; anything altered is thrown away.
  - If no relay can be reached, search says so instead of "nobody found".

[BUILT] Profiles
  - Open anyone from search: their colour, name, @handle·CODE, how many records, how many
    you both have, and their shelf as sleeves (colour and title straight away, real covers
    filled in from the Cover Art Archive as they're found). Albums you share are outlined.
  - "Add friend" puts them in your friends list; their shelf comes with them and refreshes
    whenever you open Friends.

[BUILT] Keys: backup and restore
  - "Copy account key" copies your secret key as an "nsec1..." string (the standard Nostr
    format), marked sensitive so it doesn't show in clipboard previews.
  - "Restore it": paste the key on a new phone; the account and its profile come back.
  - Lose the phone without the key and the account is gone for good (there's no "forgot
    password", because there's no server holding anything).

Technical details (for developers)
  - Signatures: BIP-340 Schnorr over secp256k1, written in plain Kotlin, checked against
    every official BIP-340 test vector and against real events fetched from a public relay.
  - Profile event: kind 33760 (an unregistered "addressable" kind, so only the newest
    version is kept), tags d=runout, t=runout, n=<each username prefix>; JSON content.
  - Keys in NIP-19 bech32 form (nsec/npub).
  - Relay client: short-lived WebSocket connections (OkHttp) to all relays in parallel;
    nothing runs in the background.
  - Verified live: a throwaway test profile was accepted by 3 of 3 public relays and found
    again by exact and prefix username search (it was set to expire after ten minutes).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.6 SENDING RECORDS TO FRIENDS (AND 300-400 MB ALBUMS)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[BUILT] The .vinyl file
  One zip file carrying a whole record:
    manifest.json   who sent it, the album (title, artist, year, quality, vinyl, notes,
                    tracks, licence) and, for friend cards, the shelf
    sleeve.jpg      the cover
    audio/01 ...    the ORIGINAL audio files, untouched (stored, not recompressed)
    checksums.json  the SHA-256 fingerprint of every audio file
  Three kinds:
    ALBUM   a record with its audio
    CARD    the record without audio: sleeve, vinyl, notes and track list
    FRIEND  your profile and shelf, to add you as a friend

[BUILT] The sharing rule
  - Audio travels for: your own music (records you pressed and marked "I made this music")
    and Creative Commons records (including every Friday Drop).
  - Everything else travels as a CARD. The button on a pulled record says which:
    "Send with audio" or "Send as a card".
  - Why: sending commercial albums whole would make Runout a music file-sharing app, which
    Google Play removes and which would put users at risk. This rule keeps the app
    publishable.

[BUILT] Safety and quality on arrival
  - Every audio file is checked against its SHA-256 fingerprint. A file damaged on the way
    is refused ("arrived damaged, ask for it to be sent again") instead of played.
    Lossless in, lossless out, proven bit for bit.
  - Files that try to write outside their folder ("zip slip"), files from a newer app
    version, records missing audio, and non-Runout files are all refused politely.
  - Size cap: 4 GB per record.
  - Paths from the sender's phone are stripped before sending.
  - Received records can be taken off the shelf ("Remove" on a pulled record).

How to send big albums with no server and no quality loss
  A 16-bit FLAC album is roughly 300-400 MB; 24-bit/96 kHz roughly 1 GB; 24/192 up to
  about 2 GB. Three ways, best first:

  1. Same room: Quick Share (Nearby Share)  [BUILT, via the share sheet]
     Straight phone to phone over Wi-Fi Direct/Bluetooth. Fully offline, any size.
     About a minute for 400 MB.

  2. Far away, both online: direct phone to phone over the internet  [PLANNED]
     The relays only pass the short "how to reach me" handshake, encrypted. The file then
     goes straight between the two phones (WebRTC data channel), in chunks, resumable if
     the connection drops, checked by SHA-256 at the end. Nothing is stored anywhere.
     Catch: on mobile data, carriers like Jio and Airtel often put phones behind shared
     addresses (carrier-grade NAT). Two such phones often can't connect directly without a
     relay server in the middle. It works best when at least one side is on home Wi-Fi.

  3. Fallback: an app that carries big files untouched  [BUILT, via the share sheet]
     Telegram (up to 2 GB per file; send as a file, not as music), WhatsApp document (up to
     2 GB), or a Google Drive link. Their storage stands in for the server you don't have.
     The friend taps the file; Runout opens it and checks every byte.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.7 PRESSING PLANT: MAKE YOUR OWN VINYL
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[BUILT]
  - Live preview of the sleeve and the record it will press.
  - Title, artist (defaults to your name) and liner notes.
  - Sleeve: six generated patterns (Orbit, Stripes, Bloom, Grid, Sunset, Type) in your
    chosen colour, or your own picture (cropped square).
  - Vinyl: black, colour, clear, splatter, marble or picture disc; eight label colours.
  - Tracks: search your shelf, open an album, tap tracks to add them (up to 40), remove
    any. Shows the running time.
  - "I made this music": then its audio can travel when you send it. Leave it off for
    mixtapes of other people's music (friends get the sleeve, vinyl and track list).
  - The label tells the truth about mixed sources: same format → the lowest bit depth and
    rate of the tracks; mixed lossless → "LOSSLESS MIX"; anything lossy → "MIXED".
  - Pressings go into your crate and can be sent or removed.

[PLANNED]
  - Sizes (7" single, 10" EP, 12" LP), gatefold and die-cut sleeves, obi strip, hype
    sticker, glow-in-the-dark vinyl, inner-sleeve art, runout-groove etchings with hidden

  - Publish your own music to Internet Archive under a CC licence so it appears in an

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.8 FRIDAY DROPS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[BUILT]
  - Every Friday, ten Creative Commons albums from Internet Archive's netlabel collection
    (thousands of free, legal albums from independent labels).
  - The same ten for everyone, without a server: the week number picks one of the 20 pages
    of the 100 most-downloaded netlabel albums, then shuffles it with the week number as the
    seed. Every phone computes the same answer.
  - Claim three a week. Claiming downloads the album straight from archive.org to your
    phone: FLAC originals if the album has them, else MP3, else Ogg, plus the cover. Progress
    shows as it downloads. Re-claiming one you already have doesn't count again.
  - The licence travels with the record ("CC BY-NC-SA 4.0" on the caption), so you're free
    to send drops on to friends with their audio.
  - A dot on the Drops button means you still have claims left this week.
  - Next Friday the allowance resets.

[PLANNED] (from the plan)
  - Curated themes instead of a ranked page ("Late-night synth", "Monsoon jazz"), chosen with
    a small "Drop Desk" script on a laptop each week.
  - Numbered copies ("#037 of 250"), tiers (Standard / Coloured / Rare) and "out of print"
    after 7 days. These need a shared counter, which means a free database (Firebase's free
    plan) or a relay-based counter.

4.9 THE COLLECTING GAME (PLANNED)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

From the plan; none of this is built yet:
  - You only play records you own; there's no search box for the whole catalog.
  - Crate digging: a back-catalog bin you flick through, 20 flips a day; about 1 in 50 is a
    Rare Find.
  - Hidden pressings: records that unlock with codes etched in the runout groove (play side

  - Later: city drops (like Record Store Day) once a city has enough users; listening

The big picture

  YOUR PHONE
  ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  Music folder ──scan──► Library (shelf) ─┐                                         │
  │  Received .vinyl records ────────────────┤                                         │

  │                                                                ▼                   │
  │                                 Player (Media3) ──► Runout DSP sink ──► speakers  │
  │                                 (in a background service)  (EQ, ReplayGain,         │
  │                                                             limiter, Pure, float)   │
  │                                                                                    │
  │  Account key (secret, stays here)    Friends list    Sound settings    Covers      │
  └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
        │ signed profile + shelf          │ .vinyl files            │ downloads, covers
        ▼                                 ▼                         ▼

  (damus, nos.lol, primal)       Telegram, WhatsApp, Drive      MusicBrainz + Cover Art
  search by username                                            Archive (covers)

The code, by area (Kotlin, about 8,000 lines in the app and 2,700 in tests)
  model/       Album, Track, audio quality, vinyl sides, licences
  library/     Folder scanning, tag reader (FLAC/MP3/WAV), cover lookup, saved shelf
  sound/       EQ maths (biquad filters), DSP chain, float audio sink, settings, player
  playback/    Background playback service, the Deck (needle, hold, flip, next/prev,
               33/45, pitch), synthesised turntable sounds
  sharing/     .vinyl packing/opening with checksums, import/export
  social/      Your profile, friends, shelves
  account/     Schnorr signatures, nsec/npub keys, Nostr events and relays, accounts and
               search
  pressing/    Pressing Plant and generated sleeve art
  drops/       Friday Drops from Internet Archive
  net/         The small HTTP client (identifies the app politely)
  ui/          Listening room, turntable, crate, record drawing, panels, people screens,
               controls

What happens when you press play (step by step)
  1. Dropping a record on the platter splits the album into sides and queues side A in the
     player. Nothing plays yet.
  2. Lowering the needle seeks to the matching track and time, plays the needle-drop sound,
     waits 0.4 s for the needle to "settle", then fades the music in.
  3. The player decodes the file. The Runout DSP sink receives each buffer, converts it to
     32-bit float, runs ReplayGain, headroom, EQ, warmth and limiter (or nothing, in Pure
     mode), and hands float audio at the file's own sample rate to Android.
  4. Every 0.2 s the deck reads the play position; the tonearm moves inward and the pixel
     screen updates.
  5. At the end of the side the player stops, the runout thump plays and the needle lifts.

Where your data lives on the phone (all in the app's private storage)
  library.json     your shelf from the folder        covers/      saved covers
  received.json    records friends sent              received/    their audio files
  drops.json       drops you claimed                 drops/       their audio files
  pressings.json   your pressings                    friends.json your friends
  settings         sound settings, crackle, account (including the secret key)
  Your music folder itself is only read, never changed.

11. HONEST LIMITS AND RISKS
==========================================================================================

  Rights (the one risk that could end the app)
    Only Creative Commons records and people's own music travel with audio. Many CC drop
    albums are NonCommercial, so the app must stay free with no ads, or those albums leave
    the drops before anything is charged. Get proper legal advice before launch.

  Not yet tested on a real phone
    Everything above passed on Robolectric. Real phones differ: especially hi-res FLAC
    decoding on specific models, USB DAC bit-perfect, Quick Share with large files, and the
    folder picker on unusual storage.

  Usernames can't be reserved
    Without a central server, two people can pick the same name. The 4-character key code
    tells them apart; friends should confirm it.

  Profiles are public
    Username, name, colour and (unless switched off) the shelf are visible to anyone.

  Lost key, lost account
    No server means no password reset. The nsec key backup is the only way back.

  Public relays are run by others
    They're free and widely used, but could change their rules, limits or close. The app
    uses three at once; the list can be changed in an update.

  Phone-to-phone over the internet isn't built, and has a network catch
    Carrier-grade NAT on mobile data (common in India) blocks many direct connections.
    Without a relay server, some pairs of phones will never connect directly; Telegram,
    WhatsApp or Drive remain the fallback.

  Internet Archive items can change or vanish
    Claimed drops are downloaded with their credits and licence, so they stay intact.

  Covers for badly tagged albums
    Won't match online; they get a typographic sleeve (or press your own).

  Distribution
    Today it's a debug-signed APK for testing. Google Play needs a $25 developer account,
    a proper signing key and Play's policies; sideloading verification is tightening
    from 2027.

13. QUESTIONS PEOPLE WILL ASK (AND ANSWERS)
==========================================================================================

  Is it a streaming app?
    No. It plays music you own, from your phone, offline. New music comes from free Friday
    drops, friends and your own pressings.

  Where does the music come from?
    Your own album folder (CD rips, Bandcamp or Qobuz downloads), free Creative Commons
    albums from Internet Archive, and records friends send.

  Is it legal?
    Playing your own files is. Free drops are Creative Commons with their licences attached.
    Sharing sends audio only for CC music and your own recordings; everything else travels
    as a card without audio. (Get legal advice before a public launch.)

  How can there be accounts without a server?
    Your account is a key on your phone. Your public profile is a signed message stored on
    free community relays (Nostr). Relays can store it but can't fake it. Search works by
    looking up tags on those relays.

  What if two people want the same username?
    Both can have it. Each handle carries four characters of its key, like @abhi·7F3A.

  What if I lose my phone?
    Restore your account on a new phone with your account key (nsec1...). Without it, the
    account can't be recovered.

  How do you send a 400 MB album with no server?
    Same room: Quick Share. Far away: Telegram, WhatsApp document or a Drive link (direct
    phone to phone over the internet is next). The file is the original audio, checked byte
    for byte on arrival.

  Does it really play hi-res?
    It outputs each file at its own sample rate in 32-bit float, up to 24-bit/192 kHz, and
    can go bit-perfect to a USB DAC on Android 14+. Whether the phone's own speaker or
    headphone jack plays it at full resolution depends on the phone.

  How does it make money?
    It doesn't, and it's designed to cost nothing to run. Many drop albums are
    NonCommercial-licensed, so any future paid feature has to be planned around that.

  Does it work offline?
    Yes, for everything except drops, cover lookups, accounts and search. Friend cards and
    Quick Share also work offline.

  Why landscape?
    The turntable and crate side by side feel like a real room. Portrait works too:
    turntable on top, crate below.

15. GLOSSARY
==========================================================================================

  AutoEq          Free headphone-correction EQ settings made from lab measurements.
  Bit-perfect     The exact digital samples from the file reach the DAC, unchanged.
  Card            A record sent without audio: sleeve, vinyl design, notes, track list.
  Claim           Taking one of this week's Friday Drops (downloads it).
  Creative        Licences that let anyone share music, with conditions such as credit
  Commons (CC)    (BY), no commercial use (NC) or share-alike (SA).
  DAC             Digital-to-analogue converter; the USB box or dongle that turns digital
                  audio into sound for headphones.
  FLAC            Lossless audio format: compressed like a zip, nothing thrown away.
  Handle          Your username plus the 4-character key code, e.g. @abhi·7F3A.
  Hi-res          Lossless audio beyond CD quality: more than 16-bit or more than 48 kHz.
  Lossless/lossy  Lossless keeps every bit (FLAC, WAV, ALAC); lossy throws some away (MP3).
  Nostr           An open protocol for signed messages stored on public relays.
  nsec / npub     Your secret key / public key written as text (nsec must stay private).
  Picture disc    A record with an image printed on the vinyl itself.
  Pressing        A record; in Runout, also a record you made in the Pressing Plant.
  Pure mode       No processing at all: the music plays exactly as recorded.
  Relay           A public server run by others that stores and forwards Nostr messages.
  ReplayGain      Loudness information in the file's tags, used to even out volume.
  Runout          The groove at the end of a side; also the app's working name.
  Schnorr         The signature scheme that proves a profile came from its owner.
  SHA-256         A file fingerprint; any change to the file changes it.
  .vinyl          Runout's record file: one zip with manifest, sleeve, audio, checksums.
